# LC 382: reservoir sampling found from the pattern, proof not yet owned

On 2026-10-27 the learner solved LC 382 Linked List Random Node, their own choice and off-track (randomness). They solved it twice, cold, 14/14 each time. **v1** counts the nodes in the constructor, then does `rand() % size` and walks to that node. Their first attempt turned the list into a cycle (`tail->next = head`), so it mutated the caller's input. The test now catches that. Asked "can the walk ever wrap?", they removed the cycle.

**Follow-up (length unknown, one pass):** their first try swapped with probability **½ at every node**, and `ans` was uninitialised. Garbage came back whenever every flip said "no". I pointed at both and asked them to compute the ½-coin probabilities and the swap chance for node 2, then node 3. They then wrote **`!(rand() % i)`, i.e. take node i with probability 1/i** (Algorithm R), and passed. But they said "I don't fully understand the maths behind what I did". I explained it with the telescoping product 1/i · i/(i+1) ⋯ (n−1)/n = 1/n, the [1,2,3] table, and the "after k nodes each holds 1/k" invariant.

**Implications**:
- Like record 0009, they reach a correct rule before they can justify it. **Re-check the proof cold** in a later session: "why does node 1, taken with probability 1, end at 1/n?" Want: survival factors that telescope.
- A new habit to reinforce: **the input belongs to the caller**. Don't relink it, and don't free it.
- I asked "which factor did the ½ coin get wrong?". **Their answer showed a misconception:** "first node 1/n, 2nd 1/(n/2), … last ½". Only the last node was right. They didn't apply the product rule (the totals sum to more than 1). I corrected it with the n=3 table (⅛, ¼, ½, garbage ⅛) and showed it is geometric, (½)^(n−i+1). The wrong factor is the take probability: ½ instead of 1/i. I then asked them to compute node 1 for n = 4 under both rules (want 1/16 and 1·½·⅔·¾ = ¼). **Multiplying independent probabilities along a path is the shaky skill.** Revisit it before any probability or expected-value problem. **Resolved in the same session:** they answered 1/16 and ¼, both correct and unaided, straight after the table. That is fluency, not yet storage, so the spaced check still stands.
